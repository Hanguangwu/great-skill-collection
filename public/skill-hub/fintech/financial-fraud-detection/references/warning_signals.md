# Complete Warning Signals Catalog

Organized by the 13-scam taxonomy from "Financial Shenanigans" (3rd Ed.).

## Category 1: Revenue Manipulation

### Scam 1 — Recording Revenue Too Early

**Quantitative signals:**
- [ ] DSO (Days Sales Outstanding) increasing faster than revenue
- [ ] DSO > 90 days without industry justification
- [ ] AR growth significantly exceeds revenue growth
- [ ] Unbilled receivables surge (30%+ YoY)
- [ ] Long-term installment receivables growing rapidly
- [ ] Operating cash flow substantially less than net income (CFFO/NI < 0.8)

**Qualitative signals:**
- [ ] Revenue recognized before contractual obligations fulfilled
- [ ] Bill-and-hold transactions, especially seller-initiated
- [ ] Revenue exceeds work completed under long-term contracts
- [ ] Extended payment terms offered (120+ days)
- [ ] Customer financing provided by seller
- [ ] Changes to revenue recognition policy (more aggressive)
- [ ] Use of percentage-of-completion without justification
- [ ] Adoption of mark-to-market accounting for non-financial contracts
- [ ] Channel stuffing / loading (quarter-end shipment spikes)
- [ ] Product shipped before customer acceptance
- [ ] Customer still has right to return/terminate
- [ ] Customer lacks ability to pay (pending financing)

### Scam 2 — Recording Fictitious Revenue

**Quantitative signals:**
- [ ] Revenue growth > 100% YoY without M&A
- [ ] AR growth far exceeding revenue growth
- [ ] Revenue / Cash from Operations ratio > 1.2 and widening
- [ ] Unusual spike in revenue in final month of quarter

**Qualitative signals:**
- [ ] Transactions lacking economic substance
- [ ] Round-trip transactions (company A buys from company B, B buys from A)
- [ ] Sales to related parties with unusual terms
- [ ] Sales to non-traditional customers
- [ ] Supplier/customer financing arrangements
- [ ] Material sales to entities controlled by management
- [ ] Revenue from sources that aren't normal business
- [ ] Side agreements that negate customer payment obligations
- [ ] "Parking" transactions (goods shipped but secretly agreed to buy back)
- [ ] Rebate/refund schemes with suppliers disguised as revenue

### Scam 3 — Boosting Income with One-Time Items

**Quantitative signals:**
- [ ] Operating income growing much faster than revenue
- [ ] Non-operating income unusually large
- [ ] Gains on asset sales classified as operating revenue
- [ ] "Other income" line item growing disproportionately

**Qualitative signals:**
- [ ] Gain on sale of business unit classified as operating income
- [ ] Sale of assets bundled with future purchase agreements
- [ ] One-time items presented as recurring revenue streams
- [ ] Discontinued operations losses hidden from operating income
- [ ] Investment gains classified as operating revenue
- [ ] Restructuring charges recycled into income
- [ ] Pension income masking operating losses

### Scam 4 — Shifting Current Expenses to Later Period

**Quantitative signals:**
- [ ] DSI (Days Sales Inventory) increasing faster than COGS
- [ ] Inventory growth far exceeding COGS growth
- [ ] CapEx surge unexplained by business needs
- [ ] Capitalized costs growing as % of revenue
- [ ] Depreciation rate very low vs. industry
- [ ] Bad debt allowance declining as % of AR
- [ ] New or unusual asset accounts appearing

**Qualitative signals:**
- [ ] Change from expensing to capitalizing costs
- [ ] Extended useful life assumptions for depreciation
- [ ] Extended amortization periods for intangibles
- [ ] Vendor rebates/reimbursements inappropriately deferred
- [ ] Software development costs capitalized too early
- [ ] Marketing/promotion costs capitalized
- [ ] Loan costs improperly amortized
- [ ] Failure to write down impaired assets
- [ ] Failure to write down obsolete inventory
- [ ] Insufficient bad debt reserves
- [ ] Insufficient loan loss reserves (financial institutions)
- [ ] Investment impairments not recognized

### Scam 5 — Using Other Methods to Hide Costs/Losses

**Quantitative signals:**
- [ ] Unexplained decreases in reserve/accrual accounts
- [ ] Operating margins improving while cash flow declines
- [ ] Accrued liabilities declining as % of operations

**Qualitative signals:**
- [ ] Not recording period-end expenses (missing invoices)
- [ ] Not recording necessary accruals
- [ ] Reversing previously accrued expenses
- [ ] Stock option backdating or improper grant-date accounting
- [ ] Supplier refunds/discounts treated as income instead of cost reduction
- [ ] Rebate income from suppliers disproportionate to purchases
- [ ] Failure to accrue for known liabilities (legal, warranty, environmental)
- [ ] Pension expense assumptions aggressively optimistic
- [ ] Tax reserves manipulated to smooth earnings

### Scam 6 — Shifting Current Revenue to Later Period

**Quantitative signals:**
- [ ] Deferred revenue growing much faster than sales
- [ ] Unearned revenue increasing without business explanation
- [ ] Cash collections growing faster than recognized revenue

**Qualitative signals:**
- [ ] "Cookie jar" reserves being filled in good times
- [ ] Deliberately delaying revenue recognition
- [ ] Underestimating percentage of completion
- [ ] Creating excessive warranty/return reserves
- [ ] Under-recording billings to defer revenue
- [ ] Building reserves to smooth earnings across periods

### Scam 7 — Shifting Future Expenses to Current Period

**Quantitative signals:**
- [ ] Large, unusual write-offs or impairment charges
- [ ] "Big bath" restructuring charges
- [ ] In-process R&D write-offs from acquisitions

**Qualitative signals:**
- [ ] Massive restructuring charge in a bad year (to make future years look better)
- [ ] Writing off assets that could have been amortized over time
- [ ] Aggressive impairment charges in a down year
- [ ] Accelerating expense recognition to inflate future earnings
- [ ] Combining multiple future expenses into one large current charge

## Category 2: Cash Flow Manipulation

### Scam 8 — Shifting Financing Inflows to Operating

**Quantitative signals:**
- [ ] CFFO growing much faster than net income
- [ ] CFFO / Net Income ratio > 1.5 without business reason
- [ ] Bank loans increasing while CFFO also rising

**Qualitative signals:**
- [ ] Bank loans recorded as customer payments (deposits as revenue)
- [ ] Proceeds from stock issuance classified as operating cash flow
- [ ] Customer deposits treated as revenue instead of liabilities
- [ ] Tax refunds from loss carryforwards artificially inflate CFFO
- [ ] Sale of receivables with recourse (should be financing, not operating)
- [ ] Securitization transactions classified as operating activities

### Scam 9 — Shifting Operating Outflows to Investing

**Quantitative signals:**
- [ ] CapEx surging without business rationale
- [ ] Capitalized costs growing faster than revenue
- [ ] Free cash flow declining while operating cash flow rises
- [ ] CFFO - CapEx (FCF) diverging from net income

**Qualitative signals:**
- [ ] Normal operating expenses classified as capital expenditures
- [ ] Software development costs aggressively capitalized
- [ ] Internal-use software costs over-capitalized
- [ ] Overhead costs included in construction/asset accounts
- [ ] Marketing and customer acquisition costs capitalized
- [ ] Maintenance costs misclassified as improvements

### Scam 10 — Boosting CFFO via Acquisitions/Disposals

**Quantitative signals:**
- [ ] FCF positive but turns negative after subtracting acquisition spending
- [ ] Receivables sold as % of CFFO is significant (>20%)
- [ ] Acquisition-related cash flows buried in operating section

**Qualitative signals:**
- [ ] Acquiring companies just before period-end to consolidate cash
- [ ] Selling receivables or other assets to boost CFFO
- [ ] Acquiring receivables through M&A to inflate operating cash flow
- [ ] Disposing of business units and classifying proceeds as operating
- [ ] Acquiring companies with large cash balances
- [ ] Acquiring companies and immediately selling their receivables

### Scam 11 — Boosting CFFO via Unsustainable Activities

**Quantitative signals:**
- [ ] Working capital changes contribute > 50% of CFFO
- [ ] Accounts payable days increasing dramatically
- [ ] Payables growing faster than expenses

**Qualitative signals:**
- [ ] Deliberately delaying payments to suppliers
- [ ] Accelerating collections from customers (pulling forward next-period cash)
- [ ] Reducing necessary expenditures (R&D, maintenance, advertising)
- [ ] One-time tax benefits inflating CFFO
- [ ] Insurance settlements or litigation proceeds in operating cash flow
- [ ] Cutting employee benefits or deferring compensation

## Category 3: Key Metrics Manipulation

### Scam 12 — Misleading Metrics That Overstate Performance

**Qualitative signals:**
- [ ] Non-GAAP metrics exclude normal, recurring costs
- [ ] Adjusted EBITDA adds back excessive items
- [ ] "Pro forma" earnings significantly higher than GAAP earnings
- [ ] Same-store sales defined in misleading way
- [ ] Customer metrics (ARPU, churn) defined inconsistently
- [ ] Book-to-bill ratio manipulated
- [ ] Order backlog or pipeline metrics inflated
- [ ] Funds from operations (FFO) for REITs manipulated
- [ ] Organic growth calculation excludes declining businesses

### Scam 13 — Distorting Balance Sheet Indicators

**Quantitative signals:**
- [ ] Debt-to-equity ratio declining while actual debt rising
- [ ] Off-balance-sheet liabilities significant
- [ ] Pension funded status worsening without disclosure
- [ ] Operating lease obligations growing (off-balance-sheet debt)

**Qualitative signals:**
- [ ] Special Purpose Entities (SPEs) used to hide debt
- [ ] Operating leases classified to avoid debt recognition
- [ ] Variable Interest Entities (VIEs) not consolidated
- [ ] Take-or-pay contracts or other off-balance-sheet commitments
- [ ] Asset values inflated (goodwill, intangibles not tested for impairment)
- [ ] Pension plan assumptions overly optimistic (discount rate, return on assets)
- [ ] Guarantees and contingent liabilities not disclosed
- [ ] Share count manipulation (dilutive securities hidden)

## Environment & Governance Red Flags (Chapter 2)

### Management & Board Structure
- [ ] Single individual dominates (CEO also Chairman)
- [ ] Family members in key management/board positions
- [ ] No checks and balances in senior management
- [ ] "Win at all costs" culture
- [ ] CEO/CFO boast about consistent performance

### Auditor Red Flags
- [ ] Same auditor for 10+ years
- [ ] Large non-audit fees paid to auditor
- [ ] Auditor is small or lacks industry expertise
- [ ] Audit partner has personal relationship with management
- [ ] History of audit disagreements or late filings

### Compensation Red Flags
- [ ] Executive compensation tied almost entirely to stock price
- [ ] Unusual bonus plans with aggressive targets
- [ ] Large insider stock sales before bad news
- [ | Option granting patterns suspicious (around lows)

### Regulatory & Legal
- [ ] SEC or regulatory inquiries
- [ ] History of restatements
- [ ] Material weaknesses in internal controls
- [ ] Related-party transactions not at arm's length
- [ ] Company uses reverse merger or SPAC to go public

## How to Use This Catalog

1. **Start with Layer 1** (Governance) — assess whether the environment enables fraud
2. **Run the Python scripts** for quantitative analysis (ratios, cash flow)
3. **Map findings to specific scams** using the numbered catalog above
4. **Cross-reference across statements** — fraud in one statement leaves footprints in others
5. **Document severity** — distinguish between aggressive accounting (within GAAP) and fraudulent accounting (GAAP violation)
